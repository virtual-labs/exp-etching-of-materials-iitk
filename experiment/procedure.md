Standard polishing of ceramic samples for metallographic analysis follows a progressive sequence from coarse grinding to fine polishing, minimizing subsurface damage while achieving a scratch-free surface suitable for etching and microscopy. Ceramics like Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, ZrO<sub>2</sub>, SiC demand diamond abrasives due to their hardness. <br>

* Section the ceramic using a diamond saw with coolant to avoid thermal damage or chipping. <br>

* Hot-mount in phenolic or epoxy resin (80-180°C) for edge retention; use glass slides or ceramic clamps for thin sections. <br>

* Ensure the sample surface is planar and securely mounted. <br>

Procedure for Polishing <br>

* <b>Coarse Grinding:</b> Use P120-P400 SiC papers or 70-40 µm diamond discs on a rotating platen (150-300 rpm). Grind with water lubrication under 20-40 N load for 1-2 min per step, progressing grit sizes until planar. Clean ultrasonically between steps. <br>

* <b>Fine Grinding:</b> Switch to 15-9 µm diamond discs or plates. Grind 1-2 min per step at 150 rpm platen/50 rpm head, reducing load to 15-25 N. Rinse thoroughly to remove all prior abrasives. <br>

* <b>Rough Polishing:</b> 6-3 µm polycrystalline diamond suspension on medium-nap cloth (e.g., Dac cloth). Polish 2-3 min at 150/60 rpm, light pressure (10-20 N). Cloths stay moist; replace if loaded. <br>

* <b>Intermediate Polishing:</b> 1-0.5 µm monocrystalline diamond on short-nap cloth. 2 min per step, reducing RPM to 120/40. Ultrasonic clean with ethanol between steps.<br> 

* <b>Fine Polishing:</b> 0.1-0.05 µm colloidal silica or alumina slurry on neoprene or chemical leather cloth. Low pressure (5-10 N), 1-2 min at 100-120 rpm. Final rinse in distilled water and dry with warm air. <br>

* Verify under optical microscope (50-100x) for scratch-free, deformation-free surface. Ultrasonic clean in acetone/ethanol if needed. <br>

Procedure for Chemical Etching <br>

* <b>Etchant Selection:</b> Choose based on ceramic-e.g., hot conc. H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> for Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (30-60 s at 80-100°C); HF/HNO<sub>3</sub> mix for ZrO<sub>2</sub>; molten Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub>-K<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> for SiC (5-10 min at 500-600°C). Prepare fresh; concentrations typically 85-98% for acids. <br>

* <b>Immersion/Exposure:</b> Place polished face-down in etchant using Teflon tweezers. Agitate gently. Time precisely (seconds to minutes) via timer. <br>

* <b>Rinsing:</b> Remove sample, rinse immediately in running deionized water (1-2 min), then dilute NaHCO<sub>3</sub> or NH<sub>4</sub>OH solution (30 s) to neutralize acid residue. <br>

* <b>Final Cleaning:</b> Ultrasonic in ethanol (1 min), rinse with distilled water, dry with warm air stream or in desiccator. <br>

* <b>Inspection:</b> Examine immediately under optical microscope (100-500x) for grain boundary relief/contrast. Re-etch if under-etched; repolish if over-etched. <br>

Procedure for Thermal Etching <br>

* <b>Sample Preparation:</b> Polish to the final stage (<0.1 µm colloidal silica). Ultrasonic clean in acetone/ethanol (2 min each), rinse with deionized water, dry thoroughly in oven at 100°C. <br>

* <b>Mount Verification:</b> Ensure no resin/polymer remains; use ceramic holders or bare specimens. Confirm planar surface under the optical microscope. <br>

* <b>Furnace Setup:</b> Preheat tube/muffle furnace to target temperature (typically 0.8-0.95 Tsinter, e.g., 1400-1600°C for Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>; 1200-1350°C for ZrO<sub>2</sub>). Use high-purity air or N2 atmosphere for Si3N4 to prevent oxidation. <br>

* <b>Heating Cycle:</b> Place sample on platinum foil or alumina crucible. Ramp at 10-20°C/min to etch temperature. Hold for 20-40 min (adjust per ceramic: shorter for fine grains). <br>

* <b>Cooling:</b> Furnace cool at 10-20°C/min to 200°C, then air cool. Avoid thermal shock. <br>

* <b>Post-Processing:</b> Ultrasonic clean in ethanol (1 min) to remove loose debris. Blow dry with compressed N2. Inspect immediately at 100-500x for grain boundary grooving/phase contrast.
